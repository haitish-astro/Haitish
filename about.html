Engineering Story

I am an Embry-Riddle Aeronautical University Honors Program aerospace engineering student with experience across systems engineering, robotics, design and simulation, verification and validation, and aerospace research. I am drawn to projects where the engineering problem is not only to make a model or write a report, but to connect mission goals, constraints, interfaces, analysis, and test evidence into one coherent system.

My current work spans Collins Aerospace RTX flight management and Data Link IV&V, HyDroQuad hybrid robotic platform research, NASA L'SPACE systems engineering, bio-inspired UAV design, laser propulsion research, and tutoring for core undergraduate engineering subjects.

Flight training gives me a practical respect for safety, procedure, and cockpit-level decision-making. Research and design work give me the other half of that picture: how ideas move from rough concepts into drawings, simulations, reviews, prototypes, and eventually hardware that people have to trust.

Working Style

How I approach engineering work.

01

Structure the problem first

I start by clarifying the mission, constraints, stakeholders, and success criteria. That makes the later CAD, analysis, and testing work easier to defend.

02

Connect models to decisions

I use models, simulations, and trade studies as decision tools, not just visuals. The goal is to understand what changes the design and what risks still need evidence.

03

Communicate clearly

Teaching, tutoring, research writing, and ambassador work have made clear communication part of my engineering process, especially when a team needs to align quickly.
