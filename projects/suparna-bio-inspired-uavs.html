SUPARNA: Bio-Inspired UAVs

What can changing wing and tail geometry offer a small aircraft? SUPARNA explores that question through design work and a broader look at bio-inspired flight.

01 / overview

Look beyond a fixed wing shape.

My design work explored morphing wings and tail sections inspired by natural flight. I developed and prototyped concepts to investigate how changing geometry could support different flight conditions.

Two ideas inform this work but are not interchangeable: morphing changes an aircraft's geometry, while an ornithopter uses wing flapping for flight. The associated AIAA paper focuses on possible applications of ornithopters; the wider SUPARNA project includes wing and tail design exploration.

The public material here describes research direction and design work. It does not claim demonstrated hover, autonomous flight, or a measured efficiency improvement.

02 / design questions

What changes when the wing changes?

A new wing shape affects more than lift. It also changes the loads, stability, actuator requirements, and control problem. The useful question is whether the benefit remains after accounting for the mechanism's added mass and complexity.

The design exploration considers wing variability and tail configuration across different flight conditions. Establishing a benefit would require controlled comparisons between configurations, followed by repeatable aerodynamic and flight testing.

03 / applications

Let the mission guide the design.

The publication examines potential roles for bio-inspired aircraft in environmental monitoring, search and rescue, communications relay, and agriculture. It also considers whether natural-looking flight could support wildlife observation with less disturbance.

These are potential applications, not demonstrated deployments. Each places different demands on the aircraft: monitoring may favor endurance, while operation near obstacles may put more emphasis on maneuverability and control. A useful design starts by choosing which problem to solve.
